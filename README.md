## Which plan fits your workload

At $5 and below, you're buying shared cores and modest RAM. Typical specs in this bracket run 1 to 2 cores, 512 MB to 2 GB of RAM, 10 to 50 GB of SSD or NVMe, and 1 to 2 TB of monthly transfer, which covers a personal VPN, a small site, a bot, or a machine to learn Linux on.

Match the plan to what runs on it:

- **Personal VPN, a bot, a static site, or a Linux sandbox:** the 1 GB plan is enough. 1 vCPU and 20 GB of disk handle a WireGuard tunnel or a single small service without strain.
- **WordPress with a cache, or Docker with a few containers:** start at 2 GB. WordPress runs comfortably on 1 to 2 GB when a caching layer and a tuned MySQL config are in place, and 35 GB of disk leaves room for images and logs.
- **A database and a web server on the same box:** take the 4 GB. Two services competing for 1 GB will swap, and swapping on shared SSD is where cheap VPS plans get their bad reputation.

Sustained CPU load gets throttled on any budget host, and overselling is common across the segment. Peak-hour benchmarks tell you more about a provider than an idle one.

## Who should not buy a $5 VPS

Skip this bracket if you need guaranteed CPU or disk IOPS, a service level agreement with credits, managed backups, or the freedom to walk away at the end of any month. The plans here are annual, unmanaged, and priced for people who can fix their own problems over SSH.

A dedicated IP is one requirement RackNerd's specials do meet, since each plan includes one IPv4 address. If a shared IP is a dealbreaker, cross Database Mart's listings off your shortlist before you compare RAM.
